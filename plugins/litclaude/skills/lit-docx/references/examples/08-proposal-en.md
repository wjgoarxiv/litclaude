# Proposal in brief

The east district has no regular place where residents can get a toaster, a coat zip or a bicycle brake fixed for free. A one-off repair event held in the central library in May 2026 drew about 260 people, who brought 310 items; 214 were repaired in five hours, and the queue had to be closed at noon. This proposal turns that single event into a monthly network: one café in each of four branch libraries, each open for one Saturday morning a month, staffed by volunteers who have been trained and insured.

::: keyfigures
- **60** café sessions over the pilot (sample)
  - Two cafés from April 2027, two more from June, monthly to July 2028
- **About 2,000** items repaired (sample)
  - 50 items a session, two thirds repaired as at the May event
- **6.4 t** kept out of disposal (sample)
  - 3.2 kg per repaired item, waste team weighing method
:::

The trust asks the district's community fund for €96,000 over eighteen months. The money pays for a part-time coordinator, tools and spare parts, volunteer training and insurance, and an evaluation at the end of the pilot. The libraries contribute the rooms at no cost.

Approval of €96,000 (sample) from the 2027 community fund round, with a start date of 1 February 2027.
:::

# Need and evidence

## What residents asked for

The May event closed with a short survey. Of 188 people who answered, 71 % said they had at least one broken item at home that they would bring to a regular café, and 58 % said the nearest paid repair shop was too expensive for the item in question. Small kitchen appliances topped the list, followed by clothing and bicycles.

## Why libraries

Branch libraries already have a meeting room, step-free access and a Saturday opening, and three of the four sit next to a bus interchange. The library service has agreed to host the cafés as part of its programme of community events, which also means the cafés appear in the library's printed and online calendar at no cost.

Four partners take part. The trust runs the network and employs the coordinator; the library service hosts the cafés and lists them in its calendar; about 40 volunteer fixers, trained in safe electrical repair, do the repairs; and the district's waste and recycling team weighs and records the items saved, so that the tonnage reported at the end uses the same method as the district's own waste statistics.

# Budget and plan

## Budget

The request covers eighteen months of running costs. Half of it pays the part-time coordinator who keeps the volunteer rota, the café calendar and the item log; the rest buys tools and spare parts, trains the fixers in safe repair, insures the sessions and pays for the publicity and the external evaluation. The libraries give the rooms at no cost, so no rent appears in the table.

## How a café runs

Each café opens from 10:00 to 13:00 on a fixed Saturday of the month. Visitors register their item at the door and wait with a coffee; a host matches them with a fixer for that category, and the visitor stays to watch and learn. Electrical items are tested for safety after every repair. Items that cannot be fixed on the day get a note on what part is needed and where to find it.

::: columns 2
### Months 1 to 3

Hire the coordinator, recruit and train the first 24 volunteers, buy tools for two libraries, and open the first two cafés in April.

### Months 4 to 9

Open the third and fourth cafés in June, add a second training round, and start the monthly weighing and reporting with the waste team.

<!-- column-break -->

### Months 10 to 15

Run all four cafés monthly, add a bicycle corner at the two libraries with outdoor space, and train three volunteers as café leads.

### Months 16 to 18

Carry out the evaluation, publish the results, and agree with the library service how the network continues without project funding.
:::

# Outcomes and evaluation

The pilot will count repairs, weigh the items saved, and survey visitors at every café. The external evaluation will compare the cost per repaired item with the cost of collecting and disposing of the same weight of waste, and will report what volunteers and visitors say they learned.

| Measure | Expected over the pilot | Counted by |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Café sessions | 60 | Library events calendar |
| Items brought | 3,000 | Registration sheet at the door |
| Items repaired | 2,000 | Fixer's note on each item |
| Weight kept out of disposal (t) | 6.4 | Waste team scales |
| Trained volunteer fixers | 40 | Training records |
| Funding per repaired item (€) | 48 | Total request over items repaired |

Source: Example Community Trust pilot plan (sample)

Besides the disposal cost, the evaluation will set the funding per repaired item, about €48, against the price of replacing each kind of item new, which for small appliances is usually higher.

The main risk is volunteer turnover. The plan trains more fixers than the cafés need at any one time and names three café leads by month fifteen, so that the network does not depend on the coordinator alone when the funding ends.
