The help desk answered 41,300 contacts in the third quarter, 12 % more than in the second, while the median first response by email fell from 9 hours to 5. Chat waiting times rose in September, when two trained agents left and the new self-service pages were still in testing.

# Summary

The quarter met two of its three targets. Email response time and customer satisfaction both improved, helped by the routing rules introduced in July that send billing questions straight to the billing group. Chat did not meet its target: the median wait in September was 3 minutes 40 seconds against a target of 2 minutes, because the desk ran two agents short for most of the month.

::: keyfigures
- **5 h** median first email response (sample)
  - July-September 2026, ticket system; target 8 h
- **3:40** median chat wait in September (sample)
  - Minutes and seconds, ticket system; target 2:00
- **84%** customer satisfaction (sample)
  - Post-contact survey, 6,120 replies; target 80 %

For the fourth quarter the team asks for two replacement hires to be advertised now rather than in January, and for the self-service pages on refunds and password resets to go live before the November sales period. Together these should bring chat waits back under target before the year's busiest weeks.

::: callout kind=key title="Request to the operations board"
Approve advertising two replacement agent posts in October (budget already in the annual plan) and the November launch date for the self-service pages.
:::

# Contact volumes

## Volume by channel

Email remains the largest channel but is no longer growing; almost all of the quarter's increase came through chat. Phone contacts fell for the third quarter in a row as more customers start in the app, where chat is one tap away.

Table 1. Contacts answered by channel (sample data)

| Channel | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 | Change (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Email | 18,900 | 19,100 | 1.1 |
| Chat | 11,200 | 15,400 | 37.5 |
| Phone | 6,800 | 6,800 | 0.0 |
| Total | 36,900 | 41,300 | 11.9 |

Source: ticket system and telephony reports, April-September 2026 (sample)

## Volume by topic

Billing and refunds made up 38 % of contacts, delivery questions 27 % and account access 19 %. Account access is the topic most suited to self-service: four in five of those contacts were password resets that the customer could complete alone once shown where to start.

A contact here is one conversation, however many messages it holds. A customer who emails and then chats about the same order counts twice, and contacts closed as spam or duplicates are not counted.

The topic mix has been stable for a year, which makes staffing easier to plan: the billing group handles a predictable share, and the general queue absorbs the seasonal swings in delivery questions around sales events and public holidays.

# Service levels

## Response and waiting times

| Median first email response (h) | 8 | 6 | 5 | 5 |

Email improved because billing questions no longer wait in the general queue. Chat waits followed staffing closely: in July the desk had 24 trained chat agents on the rota, in September 22, and the busiest hours (11:00-14:00) had no spare capacity once two agents were out.

## Satisfaction and complaints

Satisfaction held at or above target in every month. Formal complaints fell from 212 to 168, and the share that mentioned waiting time fell from 41 % to 33 %. The complaints that remained were mostly about refunds that took longer than promised, which is a finance process outside the desk's control but within its reach to explain better.

# Staffing and cost

## Agents and training

Two experienced agents left in late August for roles elsewhere in the company. Replacing a chat agent takes about ten weeks from advert to working alone on the queue: four weeks to hire, four weeks of training, and two weeks paired with a senior agent.

Table 3. Chat volume and staffing by month (sample data)

| Measure | July | August | September |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chat contacts | 4,600 | 5,000 | 5,800 |
| Trained chat agents on the rota | 24 | 24 | 22 |
| Chat contacts per agent per working day | 8.7 | 9.5 | 12.0 |

Note: 22 working days a month. Source: rota of the last working day of each month; ticket system (sample)

A replacement advertised in October would reach the queue in mid-December, which is why the request asks for the adverts now. The lead time of ten weeks is the median of the last eight hires.

## Cost per contact

Cost per contact fell from €4.40 to €4.10 because volume grew faster than the budget. This figure flatters the quarter slightly: it counts a chat contact the same as a phone call, although a chat takes on average a third less agent time.

# Self-service pages

Two pages are ready for launch: a password reset guide inside the app and a refund status page that reads the finance system directly. Together they cover the two questions customers most often could answer alone if they knew where to look. The estimate below assumes that half of password reset contacts and a third of refund status contacts never reach an agent, which is what the pages achieved with 40 customers in the September test.

Table 4. Contacts the self-service pages are expected to avoid per quarter (sample data)

| Page | Share of contacts (%) | Contacts per quarter | Resolved without an agent (%) | Contacts avoided |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Password reset | 15 | 6,300 | 50 | 3,150 |
| Refund status | 13 | 5,200 | 30 | 1,560 |
| Total | 28 | 11,500 | 41 | 4,710 |

Source: Q3 2026 contact topics; September usability test with 40 customers (sample)

Both topics usually start in chat. If only half of the avoided contacts come out of chat, about 35 fewer chats a working day, the pages take on roughly three agents' worth of chat work at September's rate of 12 chats per agent. That covers the two missing posts in normal weeks. The November peak, a quarter above normal volume, still needs the hires, which is why the pages and the posts are presented as one request.

# Outlook for the fourth quarter

The November sales period usually raises contacts by a quarter for three weeks. With two posts unfilled and no self-service pages, the team expects chat waits above 5 minutes during those weeks, more than twice the target. Advertising in October would bring the new agents onto the queue in mid-December. That is after the peak. It is still before the January returns season, which in the last two years has been almost as busy as November. The pages are the faster lever and need only the content review that legal has scheduled for the week of 20 October.

If both requests slip, the desk would need overtime of about 600 hours in November to hold chat waits near target, at roughly twice the cost per hour of regular time. The board meets once more before the sales period.
